These zines have since been incorporated into courses, the Rocky Mountain Biological Lab's undergraduate reserarch program, and a workshop I've developed for the NC State Office of Undergraduate Research. Please feel free to print and share them with anyone you think might find them helpful! I'd also love to hear if you enjoy them.

Giving and Receiving Critique on Scientific Work

Science, at its best, is a very iterative process, but most students aren't taught how to constructively critique others' work, nor how to process and act upon critiques of their own work. Histories of critique being paired with penalties, such as deducted points, can also make students hesitant to share their work in preliminary stages. I compiled the following zine through a combination of my own experiences and a meeting of the Iler-CaraDonna lab group in which other lab members shared their advice.

There is both a version suitable for printing and folding, and a plain-text version for screen-readers.




Tips on Academic Writing

I learned a lot of what I know about scientific writing from attending lab meetings, which I initially noted for my own reference, and then compiled into this zine to share with others. This is not meant to be prescriptive, but as a list of things to try if you are feeling stuck.
